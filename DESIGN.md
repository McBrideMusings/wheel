# Wheel — design

## Overview

Wheel is a native macOS and iOS app for running coding agents across projects. Each project is a space with its own grid of terminals, agent chats and web views.

## Direction

Until the MVP works end to end, the look is stock macOS: system materials, controls, type and SF Symbols, with no custom palette or avatar art. The two animations are the chat's progress shimmer on work still running and the space tabs fading out of or back into the title row as the sidebar opens or closes. Custom visual work — visual passes over the whole app — happens later as spikes.

## Layout

- The grid fills the window. The title row is drawn by the app under a transparent title bar: the traffic lights, the sidebar toggle, then the space tabs while the sidebar is closed (the window's title while it is open), and at the right end the processes, ⌘K, pin and + buttons, which stay in the row at any window width and tab count.
- **Space tabs.** Every tab is the same width: the width left between the two button groups shared out, at most 220 pt and at least 72 pt; below that the strip scrolls and keeps the active tab in view. One rounded track covers the tabs only, so a single tab leaves the rest of the row empty. A tab is its pane-stack icon, status dots and name (cut with an ellipsis); the active tab is a lighter pill, a hairline separates two inactive neighbours, and the close button shows over the tab's right end on the active or hovered tab (a space in a little window shows a back arrow there instead). A right press or control-click opens the sidebar row's space menu, and Rename Space swaps the label for an inline field with any error in red under the tab. The row's empty ground moves the window and answers a double-click as the system's title bar setting says.
- Every agent pane has the same composer: a rounded box holding the text, attachments, `/` skills and send. On the phone its + menu also holds Model and Clear chat (sends `/clear`), and a transcript scrolled away from the end shows a scroll-to-bottom arrow.
- While an agent is working and the draft is empty, the composer's send button is a Stop square beside an "esc" hint, and Esc in the composer stops the turn; typing turns the button back into send, which queues the message. A pane's header has no Stop.
- On the phone the round button at the right of the composer's lower row has one size and one place in three states: Stop while the agent works and the draft is empty, Send with text or attachments, and voice mode (a waveform) with an empty draft and an idle agent. Without `lease_claim` and `agent_voice` from wheeld the empty, idle state is a dimmed Send. The dictation mic sits to its left.
- **Voice mode on the phone** covers the agent pane it was opened in (`apple/iOS/Sources/VoiceLayer.swift`): the agent and its "space · harness" at the top with the panes button (badged with the waiting requests) at the right; the orb in the middle (blue with rings at the mic's level while listening, grey and breathing while thinking, bright and moving with the speech while speaking, dimmed while muted) with its state word under it; the reply's caption and "Speaking · tap to interrupt" below, in boxes of fixed height; a card the agent shows as a chip above the orb, which shrinks; and + / "Type instead…" / mute / end along the bottom. The panes button, a swipe left, Type instead… and the card chip shrink it to a pill (orb, state or caption, mute, end) floating at the top of the pages that keeps listening and speaking; its orb brings the layer back on the agent's pane. The first start names each model's size, source and folder with Download and Cancel.
- The controls the agent offers over itself — model, effort, permission mode — sit as a row of plain values under the composer, each opening a menu.
- While an agent has background tasks running, a strip above its composer has one row per task: a dot (green, amber once a command's output has been quiet five minutes), the task's name in semibold, its latest output line in monospace (a subagent's tool calls, last tool and tokens instead), its running time in tabular figures (`m:ss`, `h:mm:ss` from an hour) and ⤢. A click on a row opens it to its last eight lines, when it started, how long since its output grew and its output file. ⤢ covers the transcript with the full view: one tab per task, the command or brief, the last 256 KB of output following its end, Show in Transcript and Close (Esc). The transcript line of the call that started a running task says `● running 40:12`. The strip shows only what the agent's events carry; nothing shows while no task runs.
- An agent pane's header has a conversations button (a clock) left of Info. It opens a popover: a search field, "New conversation", then every conversation the agent holds for the pane's directory, newest first, the current one ticked, each with how long ago it was last worked in, and a footer saying the list includes conversations started outside Wheel (ADR-0006). A row resumes that conversation. While a turn runs or a message is queued the rows stay in place, dimmed, with the tooltip "Stop the turn first", because wheeld refuses the switch then; wheeld also refuses a conversation another agent pane is in. An empty conversation shows the last three under its placeholder, in space reserved for them, and "Show all…" opens the same popover.
- A new pane starts empty and asks what it shows; any pane can change kind from its title.
- **Phone mode toggle.** The phone's title, at the top bar's left, is the mode toggle: the shown mode's name and a ⌄ ("Spaces ⌄"). The phone has two modes, so a tap swaps Spaces and Orchestrator with no menu, and back in Spaces it shows the page it showed last; Agents mode is the Mac's alone. Under it sits the context line: in Spaces mode the shown space · page, the space's dots and the needs-you count; a tap on it opens the space switcher (the spaces list, search, the Spaces | Agents tabs and the session bar). The bar lays out from the top, so the lines under the title never move the title row or the buttons on the right. At the right sit the panes button and the ⋯. The page runs from the top bar to the bottom edge, with no dots or handle under it; a swipe across the pager or across the top bar changes page, the top bar being the strip no web page can take. The app opens on the last pane, never a home screen. Opening a pane or a space from anywhere (a tile, the space switcher, a notification) shows Spaces. A drag or a tap on an agent pane's transcript hides the keyboard.
- **Orchestrator mode.** It shows the space open on the orchestrator folder (the `orchestrator.dir` setting) and never starts one; with no such space it says "Open a space with an agent pane on <dir>". On the phone its chat is the carousel's first page, with no agent header row, and the other pages are the other panes of that space, in reading order; there is no pin sheet; on every page but the chat a 52 pt floating button returns to the chat, and a link to a pane in that space slides to that pane. The phone reopens to the last mode and page. The top bar holds the mode toggle ("Orchestrator ⌄") over the topic line, then the model menu on the chat or the shown page's name elsewhere, the queue tray when the space holds a Queue pane, the panes button, which lists this space's panes, and a Settings-only ⋯; a left swipe from the chat shows the space's first pane. The mode shows no space switcher, and the title and the ⋯ sit where they sit in every mode.
- **Phone browser pane.** One row above the page: back, forward, Home, Reload and the URL bar, plain system controls (four 36 pt SF Symbol buttons and a rounded-border text field that takes the rest), then a hairline. Back and forward grey out in place when there is no page to go to. The bar shows the page's full address, empty with its placeholder on a blank page; Return sends the text to wheeld and only the phone's browser goes there. No title, Set as home, bottom toolbar or banner.
- **Phone panes list.** The phone's top bar has a panes button left of the ⋯: two squares (`square.on.square`) with the number of panes on a grey badge and one orange dot under it while an agent in the shown session needs the person. A tap swaps the page area under the top bar (the browser row too) for one list and fills the button blue; a second tap, or opening a pane from the list, closes it. PANES: the space's panes in page order, each with its symbol, name and a detail line (a browser's page, an agent's state, an artifact's scope), a check on the shown one and an orange dot on a waiting one. AGENTS: the space's agents as full-width rounded tiles, needs you first, then stopped, working, done and idle; the backgrounded ones sit behind "Show <n> backgrounded". A tile is the agent program's grey logo (orange while it needs the person), the conversation's title, a "backgrounded" tag on a backgrounded one, the system spinner while it works and its state at the right; under them the agent's last message, two lines, three while it waits. A waiting tile has an orange border and wash, the command in monospace and three equal 44 pt buttons: Deny, Allow (filled) and an eye that opens the pane. The space switcher's Agents tab draws the same tiles with the agent's space (folder icon and name, muted) in place of the state. No tile shows a status dot or anything an agent declares through the person's own skills.

## Chat

Every agent pane draws its conversation the same way, after assistant-ui's thread.

- **Type.** Three sizes, two weights, one family. The transcript reads as the Codex app does: messages and replies at 14 pt (`chat.font.size`) with lines 1.625 times the size apart (`chat.line_height`) and 14 pt between paragraphs (`chat.paragraph_spacing`); tool, reasoning and request lines a point under it; times, counts and settings three points under it. The composer is body (13 pt). A list's bullet sits just inside the text's edge with its text two ems in (`chat.list.bullet`: `inset`, or `hanging` against the text), and its items follow with no space between them (`chat.list.spacing`). Code inside a sentence is monospace at 0.92 of the text on a rounded box (`code.inline`: `box`, `tint` for a flat fill, `plain`); a code block sits on its theme's ground inside a hairline border (`code.block.border`), 18 pt below the text above it (`code.block.spacing`). Semibold marks a name or a heading, never a whole line. Monospace is for code only: inline code, a command, a diff. Markdown headings stay within 1.25× body.
- **Turns.** The person's message sits on the right in an accent bubble (`chat.message.style`: `wash`, the default, is a dark wash of the accent colour under the normal text colour, `bubble` is grey, `accent` is the full accent colour under white text, `plain` is bare text under a "You" label; `chat.message.align` puts it left; `chat.timestamps` adds the send time under it). The reply under it has no bubble. A finished turn reads: the message, then the agent's messages in order with a "Worked for 38s ›" line (collapsed) for each stretch of work between them, and an "Edited 2 files" card when the turn changed files. 24 pt separates turns, 12 pt a message from its reply, 14 pt the parts of one reply.
- **Tool calls.** `chat.tools.summary` sets the grain. `turn` (the default) draws one "Worked for 38s" line per stretch of tool calls and thoughts between two of the agent's messages (before the first and after the last count too); it opens onto that stretch's calls and thoughts. Every message the agent writes is drawn in the main chat, never inside a stretch. `runs` draws one line per run of consecutive calls, worded from their kinds ("Ran 1 command, read 2 files"). `each` draws one quiet line per call: a status icon, the title, how long it ran, a chevron that opens the call's text and diffs. Every line opens onto the lines inside it.
- **Work in progress.** A running tool, run, turn or thought shows a spinner, a highlight sweeping across its label, and a timer that counts each second; the turn's line reads "Working · <current call> 6s". The daemon stamps every event, so the times survive a relaunch. Reduce Motion stops the sweep.
- **Reasoning.** `chat.thinking`: `collapsed` (the default) shows "Thought for 4s" and opens onto the text; `live` also shows the latest lines under "Thinking" while it streams; `hidden` draws no reasoning row.
- **Edits card.** `chat.edits.card` (default on): a finished turn whose tool calls changed files ends with a card: "Edited N files", the total lines added (green) and removed (red), and a row per file with its path and counts. It lists the first `chat.edits.shown` files (default 3); any more fold behind one row, "… 17 more files" with their combined counts, which lists every file when clicked and then reads "Show fewer". A file's row opens its own diff (changed lines only) under it, one at a time; "Collapse all" in the header, shown while a diff is open, closes every open one. A turn with no edits has no card.
- **Requests.** One at a time. The agent's oldest waiting request is a card directly above its pane's composer — the agent's own reason in plain text (with why it had to ask and the path involved, when it says), then what kind of action and the command or diff — with its choices in one group at the right: Deny, Always allow, Allow (filled). Return allows, ⌘↩ always allows, Esc denies, each tooltip naming its key, and Always allow's tooltip naming the rule it adds. "1 of N" when more wait across Wheel. The tool line it gates says it needs your input.
- **Copy.** The last reply of a finished turn has a copy button under it; earlier replies show one on hover.
- **Empty.** An empty conversation says what to ask, centred in the space the transcript has.
- **Scrolling.** The transcript follows new output until the person scrolls away from the end, and again once they scroll back.

## Artifacts

Every artifact page draws with Wheel's design system, `wheel.css`, which wheeld serves to each web pane at `.wheel/wheel.css` (source: `daemon/src/wheel.css`). A page opts in with `<link rel="stylesheet" href=".wheel/wheel.css">` and defines no colours of its own; every built-in artifact does, and a person's own artifact takes the same line.

- **Colour.** The macOS system colours (red, orange, yellow, green, teal, blue, indigo, purple, pink, gray), tuned for text in light and the system's own values in dark, plus label, secondary and tertiary text, separators, and surfaces: the window (`--bg`), a grouped box on it (`--group`), a fill (`--fill`) and a log's well (`--log-bg`). Light and dark follow the system; `<html data-theme="light|dark">` forces one.
- **Type.** The system font at body (13 px), callout (12 px), caption (11 px) and title (20 px); monospace only for code, commands and logs.
- **Groups.** Content sits in rounded boxes (`.group`) under a heading line (`.heading` with its count and a hint at the right), never one long table. A box's rows (`.group .row`) are one line at one height, cut with an ellipsis (`.one`); hover carries the full text.
- **Counts strip.** `.counts`: one cell per count, the number in its colour, a zero greyed; four to a line under 700 px.
- **Highlighted log.** `.log`: a tinted monospace well, one line per entry at one height, newest first. Commands are orange, reading teal, editing blue, agents and skills purple, speech green and italic, thinking faint; inside a command, paths are blue, quoted strings green and flags purple; a running call is orange, an error red. `.log.fade` fades the lines past the sixth, to 30%.
- **No chips.** A state is a coloured word or glyph, never a pill or a badge.
- **Controls.** Push buttons, the accent default button (`button.primary`), segmented controls (`.seg`) and text fields in the macOS shape.
- **Item actions.** An action on one item (delete, show, open) is a borderless SF Symbol button with a help label, never a text button.
- **Times.** A time shown to the person is their local clock (`3:13pm`, `Nov 16, 3:13pm`), formatted where the time zone is known, never UTC.
